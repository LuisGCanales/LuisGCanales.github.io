From swap quotes to curve nodes

Swap par rates pin down the prices of the calibration instruments. A valuation engine also needs discount factors and forwards on cash-flow dates between those instruments. Projected floating payments and the distribution of risk across tenors can therefore depend on how the curve is constructed.

The study uses a smooth synthetic reference curve to generate 15 par quotes. Calibration sees the quotes and conventions. The reference curve is used afterward to measure the accuracy of the recovered forwards.

  1. Build the cash flows. Apply the Mexico City calendar, T+2 business-day effective dates, ACT/360 accrual, 28-day coupon periods, compounded overnight F-TIIE and a two-business-day payment lag.
  2. Solve for discount factors. Set the pillars at final payment dates and use the same curve for projection and discounting. The local log-linear representation supports a sequential bootstrap. The selected natural cubic zero-rate baseline solves for all nodes together.
  3. Check the result. Verify input coverage and curve structure, then rebuild the instruments and recalculate par rates separately from the solver residuals.

Repricing & validation

In the selected cubic baseline, 15 of 15 independently reconstructed swaps passed the project’s 0.01 bp tolerance. The maximum absolute error was 4.39e-11 bp. This is an internal numerical fit check using a separate repricing path in the same codebase.

How a quote shock travels

Move one OIS input quote up and down by 1 bp and recalibrate for each move. The central difference estimates how many basis points a 28-day forward changes per basis point of input quote change. The cubic example shows effects beyond the shocked tenor and includes both positive and negative responses.

For the 30Y quote shock, 29.0% of the sampled absolute cubic forward response falls within two ACT/360 years of its pillar. On this measure it is more local than the two alternatives for that shock. Locality depends on the tenor, the measure and the sampling range. For hedge analysis, the exhibit illustrates why a single labelled maturity bucket can miss part of the curve response.

Scope & limitations

  • The quotes are synthetic. The smooth reference can favour smooth methods. Later years of the Mexico City holiday calendar are projected.
  • The same curve projects floating payments and discounts cash flows. Extrapolation outside the calibrated range is unsupported.
  • Repricing establishes fit within the project’s model. External vendor benchmarking and observed-market validation are outside the study.
  • Quote-to-curve sensitivities measure changes in rates. Monetary DV01, portfolio P&L and hedge effectiveness would require separate valuation and exposure analysis.
  • The public exhibits are a curated selection from a research implementation. The full execution snapshot and production controls are outside this release.
